The problem

The member who goes quiet rarely complains first

In a class of twenty, pacing is usually one-size: the same split, the same scaling, the same workout for the strongest and newest member in the room. Some are coasting. Some are in over their head. Either way, they stop feeling seen — and they don't tell you before they cancel.

Targets

Pace, split and load — from their bests

Distance-aware pace for run, row, bike and ski, and load targets from each member's own numbers — so a HYROX class, a metcon, or a strength day is pushing the right person at the right intensity, not a class-wide guess.

Goals

A real target, with a roadmap to it

A coach-set goal — a time, a lift, a first rep — turned into a week-by-week progression, so "get fitter" becomes something specific the member can see themselves closing in on.

Radar

The coach sees drift before it becomes a cancellation

No new workload for the coach — Waypoint flags who's fallen off their own pace, who hasn't touched a PB in weeks, or whose attendance has quietly dropped, before that member decides to leave. That's the retention lever: catching it while there's still something to do about it.
